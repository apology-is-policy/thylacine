Astra single-agent self-review: bounded local task ownership, October10.
Uninstalled Rust dispatcher, not an independent audit or native executor gate.

Tasks owns1..64preallocated optional cells. Admission checks a free cell before
calling the Future factory or allocating the Future/Arc flag. A checked global
monotonic ID prevents stale or foreign TaskId cancellation after reuse. Exhaustion
fails before invoking the factory. A failed admission drops the passed closure
normally: callers who need retry must retain inputs outside that closure. No new
kernel allowance or MM guarantee follows from the local live-task capacity.

Each task gets a distinct Arc of two atomic flags. Waker clone/wake/drop is valid
for Rust's Send+Sync Waker contract; the !Send task body and queue stay on their
creator. Old externally retained Wakers can keep only their old flag allocation
alive; they cannot reference a new occupant or keep the old Future/storage alive.
External Waker retention is not bounded by the live-task count. Native accounting
and admission for those objects are later integration obligations, not proven here.
Wake sets readiness only, never polls a Future, touches Rc task state or performs
an OS wake. Cross-thread flag safety does not imply waking a sleeping executor.

Dispatch visits at most min(budget,capacity) distinct rotating cells. It clears
readiness before polling, preserving wake-during-poll. Self-waking tasks get one
poll per visited round and cannot create an internal retry loop. Readiness is
coalesced; quiet tasks are not polled. The exact entry is detached before poll;
completion/cancellation/unwind disables its flag before dropping the Future.
Panic propagates after that owner is dropped, while other queue cells remain.
No shared RefCell borrow encloses task callbacks. Cooperative task bodies must
not block or reenter this queue; no in-poll spawning/cancellation API is claimed.
Task-owned fixed Futures retain existing abort/storage rules on cancellation.
