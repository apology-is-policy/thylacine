## Prosecution

`ready_on` with a cross-CPU target enqueued the thread onto the peer's
tree and sent a *gated* wake IPI — and nothing else.

For an **idle** target that is enough: the IPI lifts its WFI and its
`sched()` picks the new thread up. For a **busy** target it is not. The
IPI traps it into the kernel, `preempt_check_irq` runs on the
IRQ-return path, finds no `need_resched` set, and returns. The placed
thread then waits behind the running thread for **up to a full 6 ms
slice** — and if the wake IPI is suppressed (as it is during the
in-kernel test phase), until the next timer tick.

That is a latency and fairness leak, and it is a leak in exactly the
mechanism #864 exists for: the misfit push whose whole point is moving a
heavy thread onto a *busy* big core.

## Fix

`ready_on`'s cross-CPU branch sets `need_resched_set(target_cpu)` before
the notify, and — load-bearing — **that set is not gated** on the notify
flag, while the IPI is. The split is the useful part:

- `need_resched_set` is the **correctness** half. A busy target
  reschedules at its next tick regardless.
- `sched_notify_cpu` is the **promptness** half. It traps the target
  into the kernel *now* rather than at the next tick.

`g_need_resched` became a genuine cross-CPU producer as a result, so it
moved from self-CPU volatile to RELAXED atomics behind
`need_resched_{set,clear,pending}` helpers.

## Reachability

**Dormant on every v1.0 target** — `select_target_cpu` returns `prev_cpu`
on a uniform topology, so nothing ever takes the cross-CPU branch. The
runtime matrix could not reach it; the audit reached it by reasoning.
Fixed anyway, because it is the seam #864 was built for.

The same-CPU half of this gap was closed a week later by
[[chg-2026-06-11-rw11-wake-preemption]] — the 6 ms slice cliff is the
identical mechanism on the local path.
