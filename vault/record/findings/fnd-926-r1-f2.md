## Prosecution

#926 closed the handle table at the top of `exits()`. A Proc killed via
`proc_group_terminate` does not reach `exits()` — it dies at its
`el0_return_die_check` through `thread_exit_self`, which had no close. So
voluntary exit delivered EOF at exit and an involuntary one delivered it at
reap: an asymmetry with no principled basis.

## Disposition

Documented at the time as a v1.x EXITING-protocol-restructure item — and
then genuinely FIXED by [[chg-2026-07-14-68-last-thread-out-close]], whose
last-out window covers `thread_exit_self` and therefore every killed path.

Recorded here because the finding was correctly identified a month before it
was fixed, and because it shows the disposition working as intended: a P3
that names a real asymmetry gets closed by the next chunk that touches the
surface, rather than being argued away.
