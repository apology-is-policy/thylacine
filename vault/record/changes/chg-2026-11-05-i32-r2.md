Round 2 on the close of round 1, before any boot. Every new kernel test traces to a pass, so no second test extincts the boot.

**A raw signal waits its turn.** Round 1's fix read past a held byte in both modes. In raw mode that counted a byte the ring refused, so the reader got `sleep 3\r` from `sleep 30^C\r` with nothing counted: `#95`'s signature, and against `CookData` and `SignalXorByte`. The raw arm stops at that byte again, as the model says and as Linux's `n_tty` does, and a lone signal still goes. This corrects `chg-2026-11-05-i32-r1`'s "in raw mode too". The cooked half stands: a refused line is still the pending assembly, and what the write held after it was never committed, so they share the signal's disposition, uncounted, as PTY-DESIGN now says.

**The console's feed honours a cooked Ctrl-C behind a refused terminator.** The renderers re-offer what they hold, so a Ctrl-C behind a refused newline never reached ISIG and, past the renderer's bound, was the byte dropped. `cons_feed_write` reads forward to it, under one lock with the mode read. `cons.feed_intr_behind_refused_line` pins the cooked and the raw case. Nothing here has booted yet.

**Exec's file arm has its witness.** `exec.load_vma_refused_says_enomem` drives the arm `sys_execve` takes. A refused header buffer reads ENOMEM too; that buffer sits above the slab, so no lever drives it and that line has no witness.

Open and owned: a raw Ctrl-C behind a byte the feed cannot deliver waits as long as the ring stays full, and a renderer never drops what it holds, so a raw job that stops reading can keep it from the line; the same question as the serial console's paused RX, for one vote.
